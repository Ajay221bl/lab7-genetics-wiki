If the prediction were obeyed perfectly by your exact sample, how many of each would you have counted? Take the total you actually counted and split it in the predicted proportions.

## Formula

**E = (total observed) × (expected fraction)**

## Steps

1. Add all your observed counts → total N.
2. Get the expected fraction for each category from the Punnett square.
3. Multiply. Keep decimals (two places at least). E doesn't need to be a whole number.
4. **Check**: the E column adds to N.

## Examples

| Data | N | Hypothesis | E |
|---|---|---|---|
| 18 green, 5 albino | 23 | 3 : 1 | 23 × 0.75 = 17.25; 23 × 0.25 = 5.75 |
| same | 23 | 1 : 1 | 11.5; 11.5 |
| 154 purple, 46 yellow | 200 | 3 : 1 | 150; 50 |
| ear 5 (88, 27, 26, 9) | 150 | 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 | 84.38; 28.13; 28.13; 9.38 |

TA meeting: many students struggle here. The common mistake is writing the fraction (¾) or the percent (75) in the E column instead of the number.

Try it yourself. 24 seedlings, hypothesis 1 : 1. E for each? 12 and 12.
