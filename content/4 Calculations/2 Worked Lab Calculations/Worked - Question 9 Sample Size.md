> [!example] The Feynman version
> Taste one spoonful of soup and you might get all salt or none. Taste ten and you know the soup.

Compare the same proportions at two sample sizes:

| Sample | χ² vs 3 : 1 | χ² vs 1 : 1 |
|---|---|---|
| one table (23) | 0.130 | 7.348 |
| class (140) | 0.038 | 37.029 |

**Answer:** small samples vary a lot by chance, so a table's ratio can drift far from the true ratio, fit two hypotheses at once, or even fit the wrong one. With a larger sample, chance deviations average out ([[Law of Large Numbers]]) and a real difference produces a much larger χ², so the test can tell the hypotheses apart.
