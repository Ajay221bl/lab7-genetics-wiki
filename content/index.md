## The whole lab in eight sentences

1. A **gene** is a stretch of DNA that's a recipe for a protein, and proteins build traits like kernel color.
2. You carry **two copies** of each gene, one from each parent.
3. The copies can be different versions, called **alleles**; your pair is your **genotype**, and what shows is your **phenotype**.
4. Usually one working copy is enough, so the working allele is **dominant** and the broken one **recessive**.
5. **Meiosis** puts one allele of each gene into each egg or sperm, chosen by chance.
6. A **Punnett square** lists every egg against every sperm and predicts what fraction of offspring get each genotype.
7. Real offspring never match the prediction exactly, because **chance** decides which egg meets which sperm.
8. The **chi-square test** asks whether the gap between what we counted and what we predicted is small enough to be chance.
