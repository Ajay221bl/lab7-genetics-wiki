**The big question:** The Punnett square says 3 : 1. My table counted 18 : 5. Is something wrong?

## The story in plain words

Flip a fair coin 10 times. Would you be shocked by 6 heads? No. That's [[Random Chance|chance]]. A Punnett square gives [[Probability|probabilities]]; each seedling is a separate coin flip ([[Independent Event]]), so the counts wobble around the prediction.

How much wobble is normal? It depends on [[Sample Size|sample size]]. With 24 seedlings, 18 : 6 or 20 : 4 or 15 : 9 are all plausible from a true 3 : 1. With 1,000, the share settles close to ¾ ([[Law of Large Numbers]]). That's why the whole class's data give a clearer answer than one table.

To talk about these numbers you need four overlapping words: [[Ratio]] (3 : 1), [[Proportion]] (¾), [[Frequency]], [[Percent]] (75%). To combine independent genes, use the [[Product Rule]].

We count a [[Sample|sample]] (five rows) to learn about a [[Population|population]] (the whole ear and everything that cross could make).

The leftover question, 'is my wobble *normal* or *too big*?', is what the [[Chi-Square Test|chi-square test]] answers.

## Build it in order

[[Probability]] → [[Ratio]] → [[Proportion]] → [[Frequency]] → [[Percent]] → [[Random Chance]] → [[Independent Event]] → [[Product Rule]] → [[Population]] → [[Sample]] → [[Sample Size]] → [[Law of Large Numbers]]

Try it yourself. Why might one table fail to reject both 3 : 1 and 1 : 1? With a small sample, chance wobble is wide enough that both predictions are 'close enough'.
