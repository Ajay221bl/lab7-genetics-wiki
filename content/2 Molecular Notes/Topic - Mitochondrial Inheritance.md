> [!abstract] The big question
> Is all your DNA half from Mom and half from Dad?

## The story in plain words

No! Your nuclear chromosomes are half and half. But your cells also have power plants, [[Mitochondrion|mitochondria]], and they carry their own tiny DNA ([[Mitochondrial DNA|mtDNA]], 37 genes).

Why do they have their own DNA? Because they used to be free-living bacteria ([[Prokaryote|prokaryotes]]) that got swallowed by a bigger cell and stayed on as power suppliers ([[Endosymbiotic Theory]]).

Why only from Mom? The [[Egg|egg]] is a big suitcase packed with cytoplasm and mitochondria; the [[Sperm|sperm]] is a courier carrying only chromosomes, and any sperm mitochondria are destroyed ([[Maternal Inheritance]]).

Because mtDNA never shuffles ([[Genetic Recombination|no recombination]]), it's passed down nearly unchanged, changing only by slow [[Mutation|mutation]]. That makes it a family-line fingerprint: useful for [[Genealogy|genealogy]], [[Forensic Identification|forensics]] and [[Evolution|evolution]]. Its mutations can cause diseases of energy-hungry tissues: [[Leber Hereditary Optic Neuropathy]], [[Nonsyndromic Deafness]].

## Build it in order

[[Prokaryote]] → [[Eukaryote]] → [[Endosymbiotic Theory]] → [[Mitochondrion]] → [[ATP]] → [[Aerobic Respiration]] → [[Mitochondrial DNA]] → [[tRNA]] → [[rRNA]] → [[Oogenesis]] → [[Spermatogenesis]] → [[Maternal Inheritance]] → [[Genealogy]] → [[Forensic Identification]] → [[Leber Hereditary Optic Neuropathy]] → [[Nonsyndromic Deafness]]

> [!question]- Check yourself: Can you inherit your paternal grandmother's mtDNA?
> **Answer:** No. Your father got it, but he can't pass it on; yours comes from your mother's line.
