> [!abstract] The big question
> Why can you read genetics right off a corn cob?

## The story in plain words

A corn plant has its male flowers on top (the [[Tassel|tassel]], making [[Pollen|pollen]]) and its female flowers lower down (the [[Corn Ear|ear]]). Each potential kernel on the ear is an [[Ovule|ovule]] with its own [[Silk|silk]]. A pollen grain lands on a silk, grows down it, and fertilizes that one ovule, which grows into one [[Kernel|kernel]].

So **every kernel is its own child**, from its own fertilization. An ear with 400 kernels is a family of 400 siblings you can count in minutes. And kernel color ([[Purple Kernel]] / [[Yellow Kernel]]) and shape ([[Smooth Kernel]] / [[Wrinkled Kernel]]) show that kernel's own genotype.

Why wrinkled? hh kernels can't turn [[Sugar|sugar]] into [[Starch|starch]]. Sugar holds water, so when the kernel dries it shrivels: sweet corn. Starchy kernels keep their shape: field corn.

Plant kernels from one cross and you get seedlings: [[Green Seedling|green]] (G_) or [[Albino Seedling|albino]] (gg), grown in a [[Seedling Tray|tray]].

## Build it in order

[[Corn]] → [[Tassel]] → [[Pollen]] → [[Corn Ear]] → [[Silk]] → [[Ovule]] → [[Kernel]] → [[Lab 7 Letter Key]] → [[Purple Kernel]] → [[Yellow Kernel]] → [[Starch]] → [[Sugar]] → [[Smooth Kernel]] → [[Wrinkled Kernel]] → [[Green Seedling]] → [[Albino Seedling]] → [[Seedling Tray]] → [[Counting Five Rows]]

> [!question]- Check yourself: An ear has a gap with no kernel. Biologically, what likely happened?
> **Answer:** That ovule's silk was never pollinated, so no fertilization, no kernel.
