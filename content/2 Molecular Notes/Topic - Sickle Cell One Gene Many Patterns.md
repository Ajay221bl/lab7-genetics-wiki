> [!abstract] The big question
> How can the same gene be called recessive, codominant AND incomplete dominant? (Study guide Q8.)

## The story in plain words

The label depends on **what level you look at**:

- **Whole-person disease**: you need two Hb^S to get [[Sickle Cell Anemia|sickle cell anemia]] → *recessive*.
- **Molecules**: a carrier's red cells contain both normal and sickle [[Hemoglobin|hemoglobin]] → both alleles expressed → *codominant*.
- **Cells under stress**: carriers have some sickling at low oxygen, in between normal and diseased → *incomplete dominance*.
- **Survival where [[Malaria|malaria]] is common**: carriers do best of all → [[Heterozygote Advantage|heterozygote advantage]].

The mutation is **helpful, harmful or neutral** depending on dose and environment. [[Natural Selection|Natural selection]] keeps it because carriers survive malaria, even though some of their children get the disease. Evolution is a tinkerer, not an engineer ([[Evolution]]).

[[Protein]] → [[Hemoglobin]] → [[Mutation]] → [[Sickle Cell Anemia]] → [[Sickle Cell Trait]] → [[Anemia]] → [[Malaria]] → [[Heterozygote Advantage]] → [[Natural Selection]] → [[Evolution]]

> [!question]- Check yourself: If malaria disappeared from a region, what would you predict for the Hb^S allele over many generations?
> **Answer:** It would slowly become rarer, since carriers lose their advantage while Hb^S Hb^S people still suffer.
