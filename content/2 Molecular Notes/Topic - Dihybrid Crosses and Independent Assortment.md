> [!abstract] The big question
> What happens when you track two genes, kernel color and kernel shape, at the same time?

## The story in plain words

Think of each gamete as flipping **two** coins: a color coin (R or r) and a shape coin (H or h). If the genes are on different chromosomes, the coins don't influence each other ([[Law of Independent Assortment]]). So an RrHh plant makes four gamete types equally: **RH, Rh, rH, rh** (use [[FOIL Method|FOIL]]).

Cross RrHh × RrHh: 4 gametes × 4 gametes = 16 equally likely boxes. Group them by looks:
- purple smooth (R_H_): 9
- purple wrinkled (R_hh): 3
- yellow smooth (rrH_): 3
- yellow wrinkled (rrhh): 1

That's the [[9-3-3-1 Ratio]]. Shortcut with the [[Product Rule]]: ¾ × ¾ = 9/16, ¾ × ¼ = 3/16, ¼ × ¾ = 3/16, ¼ × ¼ = 1/16.

Cross RrHh × rrhh (a dihybrid [[Test Cross]]): the rrhh parent only gives rh, so the offspring reveal the four gametes of the other parent equally: [[1-1-1-1 Ratio]].

## The #1 mistake

Writing gametes like 'Rr' or 'Hh'. Every gamete needs **one color letter and one shape letter**. See [[Calc 02 - Writing Gametes]].
