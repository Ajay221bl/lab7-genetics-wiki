Every box in a Punnett square is equally likely because each parent makes each kind of gamete equally often, and which egg meets which sperm is random.

Gg makes G and g eggs 50:50, and G and g pollen 50:50. Picking an egg is a coin flip; picking a pollen grain is another coin flip. Two flips have four equally likely results: HH, HT, TH, TT. Those are the four boxes: GG, Gg, gG, gg, each ¼.

That's why counting boxes gives probabilities. It only works because the boxes are equal; if one gamete were more common (say, in [[Gene Linkage|linked genes]]), the boxes would be unequal and the square would lie.

Try it yourself. Why does Gg × Gg give Gg twice as often as GG? Two boxes (Gg and gG) make Gg, only one makes GG.
