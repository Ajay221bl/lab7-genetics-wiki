A bigger sample gives a clearer answer because chance wobble shrinks relative to the total, so real differences stand out and fake ones fade.

Same shape of data, different sizes. 18:5 (n = 23) vs 1:1 gives χ² = 7.348. Class data 106:34 (n = 140) vs 1:1 gives χ² = 37.029. The proportions are similar, but the bigger sample makes the verdict far more decisive. Gaps grow in proportion to n, and χ² grows with them.

![[Sample size wobble.png|700]]

*As n grows, the observed share settles near the true value.*

This is exactly question 9: pooling class data (Table B) makes the conclusion about the parents much more confident.

Try it yourself. Why might one table fail to reject 1:1 while the whole class rejects it? The single table is too small to see the difference.
