A gamete carries one allele of each gene because meiosis separates the two homologous chromosomes into different cells, and each gamete gets just one of the pair.

Picture a pair of shoes: left and right. Meiosis is like packing for two different trips; each suitcase gets exactly one shoe of each pair. A Gg plant makes suitcases with G and suitcases with g, half and half. That's Mendel's [[Law of Segregation]].

![[Meiosis sorts alleles.png|700]]

*A Gg cell copies its chromosomes, then two divisions leave four gametes: two carry G, two carry g.*

Why does this matter? Because it's where the ½ comes from in every Punnett square. Each parent's alleles go into its gametes with equal odds, and that's the only reason we can predict ratios at all.

Try it yourself. What gametes does an Rr plant make? R and r, half each.
