The sickle cell allele survives in malaria regions because carriers (HbᴬHbˢ) resist malaria better than people with two normal alleles, so carriers leave more children, keeping the allele common even though two copies cause disease.

Lay out the three genotypes where malaria is common:

| Genotype | Malaria | Sickle cell anemia | Overall survival |
|---|---|---|---|
| HbᴬHbᴬ | vulnerable | no | lower |
| HbᴬHbˢ | resistant | no (trait only) | **highest** |
| HbˢHbˢ | resistant | yes | lowest |

Carriers win. So every generation, the Hbˢ alleles carried by healthy, malaria-resistant carriers are passed on, balancing the ones lost in HbˢHbˢ children. This is [[Heterozygote Advantage|heterozygote advantage]] and an example of [[Natural Selection|natural selection]] trading one cost for a bigger benefit. Where there's no malaria, there's no benefit, and the allele slowly becomes rarer.

Try it yourself. Is the sickle allele "good" or "bad"? It depends on the environment.
