The null hypothesis says "no difference" because that's the one claim that makes an exact, checkable prediction: if it's true, you know precisely what counts to expect.

"The ratio is 3:1" gives you E = 17.25 and 5.75. "The ratio is something other than 3:1" gives you... nothing to compute. You can only measure distance from a specific point. So we start with the specific claim and ask if the data is far enough from it to throw it out.

It's the courtroom again: you can't prove innocence in general, but you can check whether the evidence is strong enough to overturn the presumption.

Try it yourself. Why not make H₀ "the ratio is not 3:1"? It predicts no specific counts, so you'd have nothing to compare against.
