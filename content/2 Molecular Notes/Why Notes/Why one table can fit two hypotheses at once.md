One table can fit two hypotheses because with small samples, the wobble from chance is wide enough to cover more than one ratio.

The tray of 23: 18 green : 5 albino. Against 3:1, χ² = 0.130. Against 2:1, χ² ≈ 1.39. Both are under 3.841, so neither is rejected. The data simply isn't sharp enough to tell them apart. (Against 1:1 it's 7.348, so that one **is** ruled out.)

That's why the lab tests specific a priori hypotheses from genetics (3:1 for Gg × Gg, 1:1 for Gg × gg), and why the class data matters: with 140 seedlings, the ratios separate.

Try it yourself. What could you do to decide between 3:1 and 2:1? Count many more seedlings.
