The working allele is usually dominant because one working copy usually makes enough of its protein to do the job, so the broken copy's absence goes unnoticed.

Think of a kitchen with two cooks. If one cook calls in sick, the other can still make dinner, maybe a bit slower, but the food comes out the same. That's Gg: one G makes enough chlorophyll enzyme, and the seedling looks just as green as GG.

![[Dominance as enzyme dose.png|700]]

*Two working copies, one working copy, zero working copies. One copy already makes enough product to look normal.*

Only when **both** cooks are out (gg) does dinner fail: no chlorophyll, albino. So the broken allele is recessive. Its effect only shows when there's nothing to cover for it.

The trap: dominance isn't one allele "bossing" the other. It's about whether one dose is enough. When one dose is **not** enough (a half-dose gives half the color), you get [[Incomplete Dominance|incomplete dominance]] instead.

Try it yourself. Why is purple (R) dominant over yellow (r)? One R makes enough enzyme for full purple pigment.
