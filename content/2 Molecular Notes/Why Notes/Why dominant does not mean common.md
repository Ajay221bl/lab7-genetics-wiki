title: "Why dominant does not mean common"

Dominant describes how an allele behaves inside one person (does one copy show?), not how many people carry it. How common an allele is depends on history, mutation and natural selection.

Two separate questions: "Does one copy show?" (dominance) and "How many people have it?" (frequency). They're unrelated. Huntington disease is dominant but rare. Six fingers (polydactyly) is dominant and rare. Blood type O is recessive and the most common type in many populations.

Students often think "dominant traits will take over". They won't. A dominant allele that kills before reproduction disappears; a recessive one that helps carriers (sickle cell where malaria is common) can spread.

Try it yourself. If 90% of a population has a recessive trait, is that a contradiction? No. Dominance says nothing about frequency.
