A big χ² means a poor fit because χ² is built by adding up the (squared, scaled) gaps between observed and expected; big gaps make a big total.

If the data matched exactly, every O − E would be 0, and χ² would be 0. Every mismatch adds to it. So χ² is a mismatch meter: low means close fit, high means far off.

The trap: students sometimes think a high number is "good" (like a test score). Here high is bad news for H₀.

Try it yourself. Tray vs 3:1 = 0.130, tray vs 1:1 = 7.348. Which hypothesis fits better? 3:1.
