An albino seedling (gg) means both parents carry g because a seedling gets exactly one allele from each parent; to have two g's, each parent must have given one.

Then reason forward. Both parents are green (they grew and made seeds), so each is Gg (or one could be gg, but albino plants die before reproducing). So the likely cross is Gg × Gg, predicting 3:1, and that's the hypothesis to test against the tray.

The tray of 18:5 supports it (χ² 0.130 vs 3:1) and rules out Gg × gg (χ² 7.348 vs 1:1).

Try it yourself. A tray with zero albinos out of 24. Could the parents still be Gg × Gg? Possible but unlikely (chance about (¾)²⁴ ≈ 0.1%); more likely at least one is GG.
