We need a statistical test because our eyes can't judge whether a gap is chance or real; a test gives a fair, agreed rule so everyone reaches the same verdict from the same data.

Look at two trays: 18:5 and 15:8. Both are "kind of" 3:1. Which fits? People argue, and they tend to see what they hoped for. The test replaces gut feeling with a fixed rule: compute χ², compare it with the critical value, done.

It also answers a deeper question: "how surprised should I be?" A test tells you how often chance alone would produce a gap that big. If it's rare (less than 5% of the time), you stop blaming chance.

Try it yourself. Against 3:1, what are the χ² values of 18:5 and 15:8? 0.130 and ≈ 1.17. Both below 3.841, so both fit.
