About 1 in 20 honest tables will still reject because the 0.05 level means: if H₀ is true, chance alone pushes χ² past the critical value 5% of the time.

![[False alarms happen about 5 percent of the time.png|700]]

*Many simulated tables testing a true 3:1; about 5% land past the line.*

So in a class of 20 tables all testing a true 3:1, expect about one to reject. That's not new genetics. It's a [[Type I Error]], built into the 0.05 choice. If that table also double-checks its counts and still rejects, it's a good moment to discuss false alarms.

Try it yourself. Two of 24 tables reject 3:1. Should the class conclude the ratio is wrong? No: the class total is the better test.
