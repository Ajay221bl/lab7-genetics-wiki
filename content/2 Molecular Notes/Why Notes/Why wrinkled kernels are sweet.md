Wrinkled kernels are sweet because hh kernels can't turn sugar into starch, so the sugar stays as sugar, which also pulls in water that later dries out, wrinkling the kernel.

Follow the chain: gene → enzyme → chemistry → what you see.

1. H makes an enzyme that strings sugar into [[Starch|starch]].
2. hh: no working enzyme → sugar stays loose → tastes sweet.
3. Loose sugar draws water in while the kernel develops (osmosis).
4. As the kernel dries, that water leaves and the kernel shrinks into wrinkles.

Hh: one working copy makes enough enzyme, so the kernel is smooth and starchy. That's why smooth is dominant. Sweet corn varieties are built on alleles like this one.

Try it yourself. Which genotypes taste starchy? HH and Hh.
