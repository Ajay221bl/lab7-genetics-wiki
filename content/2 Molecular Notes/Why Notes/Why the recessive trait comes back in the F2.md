title: "Why the recessive trait comes back in the F2"

---

The recessive trait vanishes in the F1 because every F1 has one dominant allele hiding the recessive one. It returns in the F2 because two hidden carriers can each pass on the recessive allele, making some aa offspring.

Walk it through. P generation: RR (purple) × rr (yellow). Every F1 gets R from one parent and r from the other: all Rr, all purple. The yellow didn't disappear; it's hiding.

Now cross F1 × F1: Rr × Rr. Each parent passes r half the time, so ½ × ½ = ¼ of the F2 are rr: yellow again. That's what puzzled people before Mendel and what proved that alleles are separate particles, not blended paint.

Try it yourself. In ear 1 (Rr × Rr F2), what share of kernels should be yellow? About ¼.
