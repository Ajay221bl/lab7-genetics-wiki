You have two copies of every gene (on autosomes) because you were built from two cells: one egg from your mother and one sperm from your father, each carrying one full set of chromosomes.

Start from the problem. Reproduction mixes two parents. If each parent just handed over all of their DNA, the child would have double, the grandchild quadruple, and so on. Nature's fix: each parent first halves their set ([[Meiosis]]) to make a [[Haploid|haploid]] gamete with one copy of each chromosome. Two halves meet at [[Fertilization|fertilization]] and the [[Diploid|diploid]] number is restored.

So you hold two "editions" of the same instruction book: Mom's and Dad's. The same chapter (gene) appears in both, at the same place ([[Locus]]), but the wording can differ ([[Allele]]).

The exception: in males, genes on the X have only one copy, because the partner is a Y ([[Why X-linked traits show up more in males]]).

Try it yourself. A corn seedling's genotype is Gg. Where did each letter come from? One from the egg (ovule), one from the pollen.
