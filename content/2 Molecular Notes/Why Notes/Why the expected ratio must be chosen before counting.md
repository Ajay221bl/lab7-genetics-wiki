The expected ratio must be chosen before counting because if you pick it after seeing the data, you can always find some ratio that fits, and the test becomes meaningless.

It's like the Texas sharpshooter: fire at a barn, then paint the target around the bullet holes. Perfect aim, every time, and it proves nothing. The ratio has to come from genetics (the cross you think happened), not from the counts.

Try it yourself. A student counts 130:70, then tests 2:1 "because it looks like it". What's wrong? The hypothesis came from the data; it should come from the cross (a priori).
