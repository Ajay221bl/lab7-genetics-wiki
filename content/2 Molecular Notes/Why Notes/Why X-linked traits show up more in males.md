X-linked recessive traits show up more in males because males have only one X: a single recessive allele there has no second copy to cover for it.

Males are XY. The Y carries very few genes and no backup for the X genes. So a male with Xᵇ Y shows the trait. A female needs Xᵇ Xᵇ, two copies, which is much less likely. A female with one is a [[Carrier|carrier]].

![[X-linked cross.png|700]]

*A carrier mother (XᴮXᵇ) and a normal father (XᴮY): half the sons are color-blind, no daughters are, half the daughters are carriers.*

Another pattern: a father never passes his X to his sons (he gives them his Y). So X-linked traits pass from father → daughter → grandson, skipping a generation in appearance.

Try it yourself. A color-blind father and a non-carrier mother. Chance a son is color-blind? 0%: sons get Dad's Y.
