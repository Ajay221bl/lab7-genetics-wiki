> [!abstract] The big question
> Given a description of parents and offspring, how do you name the pattern of inheritance? (Exam skill, slide 2.)

## The story in plain words

Put two different alleles in one body and ask: **what does that heterozygote look like?**

| If the heterozygote… | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| looks exactly like one parent | [[Complete Dominance]] | Rr purple; Bb brown eyes |
| is a blend in between | [[Incomplete Dominance]] | red × white → pink; black × white chicken → gray |
| shows both, side by side | [[Codominance]] | AB blood; black × white chicken → speckled |
| depends on whether it's male or female | [[Sex-Linked Inheritance]] | color blindness, hemophilia |

Two add-ons: a gene can have [[Multiple Alleles]] (like [[ABO Blood Type]]), and sometimes the heterozygote is the fittest of all ([[Heterozygote Advantage]]).

## Worked examples from the slides

- Slide 17: black chicken × white chicken → all speckled → **codominance**.
- Slide 18-19: if feather color were incomplete dominance → **gray**.
- Study guide Q4: curly, straight, wavy hair in mice → three phenotypes, wavy in between → **incomplete dominance**. See [[Worked - Study Guide Q4 Mouse Hair]].
- Study guide Q6: blood type → **codominance with multiple alleles**. See [[Worked - Study Guide Q6 Blood Type]].
- Study guide Q7: color-blind woman × normal man → **X-linked recessive**. See [[Worked - Study Guide Q7 Color Blindness]].

> [!question]- Check yourself: Roan cattle have red and white hairs mixed together. Which pattern?
> **Answer:** Codominance (both colors show, unblended).
