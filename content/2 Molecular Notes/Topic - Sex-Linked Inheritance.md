> [!abstract] The big question
> Why are color blindness and hemophilia so much more common in men?

## The story in plain words

Women have two X chromosomes; men have one X and a small Y that carries almost no matching genes ([[Sex Chromosomes]]). A recessive allele on an X ([[X-Linked Trait]]) is like a typo in a book:
- A woman has two copies of the X book. A typo in one is covered by the clean copy, so she's a [[Carrier|carrier]] with normal vision.
- A man has one copy. No backup. The typo shows.

Also, a son gets his X from Mom (Dad gave him the Y). So X-linked traits go from mother to son, never father to son.

## Worked example (study guide Q7)

X^b X^b (color-blind woman) × X^B Y (normal man). Daughters get X^B from Dad + X^b from Mom → carriers, normal vision. Sons get Y from Dad + X^b from Mom → color-blind. See [[Worked - Study Guide Q7 Color Blindness]].

## Build it in order

[[Autosome]] → [[Sex Chromosomes]] → [[Sex-Linked Inheritance]] → [[X-Linked Trait]] → [[Y-Linked Trait]] → [[Carrier]] → [[Color Blindness]] → [[Hemophilia]]

> [!question]- Check yourself: A carrier woman (X^B X^b) × normal man (X^B Y). Chance a son is color-blind?
> **Answer:** ½ of sons.
