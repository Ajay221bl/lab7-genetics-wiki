> [!abstract] In one sentence
> A purebred (true-breeding) individual is homozygous for a trait, so when bred with its own kind it always produces offspring with that same trait.

> [!example] Explain it like I'm new (the Feynman way)
> A purebred is a reliable photocopier: it always passes the same allele, so its kids look like it (when bred to its own kind).

## Build it from first principles

Self-pollinate purebred purple corn (RR) and every kernel is purple, generation after generation. Mendel started every experiment with purebred plants so he knew the parents' genotypes for sure.

The [[P Generation|P generation]] of a classic cross is two purebreds: **RR × rr**.

> [!question]- Test yourself: Is a purebred homozygous or heterozygous?
> **Answer:** Homozygous.
