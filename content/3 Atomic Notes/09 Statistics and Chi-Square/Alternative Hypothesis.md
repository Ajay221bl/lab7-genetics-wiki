> [!abstract] In one sentence
> An alternative hypothesis (H₁, H₂, …) states a possible reason the data would differ significantly from the null hypothesis; you turn to it if H₀ is rejected.

> [!example] Explain it like I'm new (the Feynman way)
> The alternative is the list of other suspects if the 'just luck' story falls apart.

## Build it from first principles

The manual asks for 'null (H₀) and alternative (H₁, H₂, etc.) hypotheses' on p. 134. The answer key gives two alternatives for each ear:

- **H₁**: the counts differ from the expected ratio because the parents were a **different cross** (e.g. Rr × rr instead of Rr × Rr).
- **H₂**: they differ for a reason **other than the cross**: the genes are linked and don't assort independently (ear 5), some kernels survive less well, or counting error.

Write them before you calculate.

> [!question]- Test yourself: Give an H₂ for ear 5.
> **Answer:** The two genes are linked (not independently assorting), or counting/survival differences.
