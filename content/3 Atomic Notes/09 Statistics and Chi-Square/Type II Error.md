A Type II error is failing to reject H₀ when it was actually false: missing a real difference.

Small samples cause these. With 23 seedlings, a real ratio of 2:1 and a predicted 3:1 look alike, so the test can't tell them apart. Bigger samples shrink this risk ([[Why a bigger sample gives a clearer answer]]).

The tray of 18 green : 5 albino fits 3:1 (χ² = 0.130) but it also fits 2:1 (χ² ≈ 1.39, still under 3.841), which is why "fail to reject" never means "proved" ([[Why one table can fit two hypotheses at once]]).

Try it yourself. How do you lower the chance of a Type II error without raising Type I? Count more.
