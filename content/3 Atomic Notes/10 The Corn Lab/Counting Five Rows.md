> [!abstract] In one sentence
> In Lab 7 each student counts the kernels in five rows of one ear (not the whole cob), giving a sample of about 100-250 kernels.

> [!example] Explain it like I'm new (the Feynman way)
> Taste a spoonful, not the whole pot: five rows is a big enough sample.

## Build it from first principles

Why only five rows? It's a [[Sample|sample]] of the population (the whole ear), and it's large enough for chi-square while leaving time for the math. On a short ear, 6 rows is fine. The TA meeting deliberately avoids 'count 100 kernels', because harder math is better remembered.

Count rows straight down the length of the ear; mark your starting row.

> [!question]- Test yourself: About how many kernels should five rows give?
> **Answer:** 100–250.
