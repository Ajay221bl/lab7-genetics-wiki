Three Greek letters appear in Lab 7: χ (chi, said "ky"), α (alpha) and Σ (capital sigma).

- **χ²** ("ky-square"): the chi-square statistic, your one-number summary of how far the counts are from the prediction.
- **α** ("alpha"): the significance level, the false-alarm rate you agree to accept, 0.05 in this lab.
- **Σ** ("sigma"): "add these up".

Other symbols you'll meet: **×** in a cross means "crossed with"; **:** in a ratio means "to"; **p** is the p-value; **df** is degrees of freedom; **k** is the number of categories; **O** and **E** are observed and expected counts.

Why Greek? Habit, mostly. Scientists ran out of Latin letters long ago, and Greek gives them a second alphabet. Nothing mysterious hides behind them.

Try it yourself. Say "χ² = 0.130, df = 1, p > 0.05" out loud in plain English. "The chi-square value is 0.130, with one degree of freedom, and the chance of a gap this big from luck alone is more than 5%."
