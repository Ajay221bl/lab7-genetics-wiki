A decimal is a fraction with the division actually done (¾ = 0.75), and a percent is that decimal counted out of 100 (0.75 = 75%).

A fraction is a division you haven't done yet. ¾ literally means "3 divided by 4". Do it on a calculator: 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75. That's the decimal.

"Per cent" is Latin for "per hundred". If 0.75 of the plants are green, then out of every 100 plants, 75 are green: 75%. So to go from a decimal to a percent, multiply by 100. To go back, divide by 100.

Why do we need all three forms? Because each job prefers one. Percents are easiest to say out loud ("75% green"). Decimals are what you multiply with on a calculator (23 × 0.75 = 17.25). Fractions show where the number came from (3 boxes out of 4 in a Punnett square).

The trap: typing 75 instead of 0.75 when you multiply. 23 × 75 = 1,725 expected green plants from a tray of 23! If an answer is bigger than your total, you used the percent instead of the decimal.

Try it yourself. Write 3/16 as a decimal and a percent. 3 ÷ 16 = 0.1875, which is 18.75%.
