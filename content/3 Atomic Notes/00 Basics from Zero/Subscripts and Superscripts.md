Small letters or numbers written below a symbol (subscripts, like H₀ or F₁) or above it (superscripts, like Xᴮ or Iᴬ) are labels that tell you which one of something you mean.

Biology and statistics borrow this from math to keep names short:

- **H₀** is "hypothesis number zero", the null hypothesis. H₁ and H₂ are alternatives.
- **F₁, F₂** are the first and second filial (offspring) generations.
- **Xᴮ, Xᵇ** are X chromosomes carrying the B or b allele: the allele rides on the X, so it's written on it.
- **Iᴬ, Iᴮ** are the blood type alleles. The "I" is the gene; the superscript says which version.
- **Hbᴬ, Hbˢ** are normal and sickle hemoglobin alleles.
- **χ²** is different: that ² really is part of the name (it's built from squared terms).

The trap: reading a superscript as an exponent. Xᴮ isn't "X to the power B". It's just a label.

Try it yourself. What does Xᵇ Y mean? A male whose single X carries the recessive b allele.
