A fraction is a part of a whole written as top over bottom (¾ means 3 parts out of 4), and a ratio compares groups to each other (3 : 1 means 3 of one kind for every 1 of the other).

Picture a pizza cut into 4 equal slices. You eat 3. You ate ¾ of the pizza: the bottom number (4) says how many equal pieces the whole was cut into, and the top number (3) says how many you're talking about.

Now picture a tray of seedlings where, for every 3 green plants, there's 1 albino. That's a **ratio**, 3 : 1. Notice it doesn't tell you how many plants there are. It could be 3 and 1, or 30 and 10, or 18 and 6. A ratio is a recipe for proportions, not a headcount.

How do you get from a ratio to fractions? Ask: how many "parts" in total? 3 + 1 = 4. So green is 3 of every 4 (¾) and albino is 1 of every 4 (¼). That one move, **add the parts, then put each part over the total**, turns every ratio in this lab into fractions:

| Ratio | Parts add to | Fractions |
|---|---|---|
| 3 : 1 | 4 | ¾, ¼ |
| 1 : 1 | 2 | ½, ½ |
| 1 : 2 : 1 | 4 | ¼, ½, ¼ |
| 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 | 16 | 9/16, 3/16, 3/16, 1/16 |

*The same three ratios drawn as slices of a whole.*

The trap: reading "3 : 1" as "3 out of 1" or as ⅓. It means 3 for every 1, so the albino share is ¼, not ⅓.

Try it yourself. A cross gives 1 purple : 1 yellow. What fraction is yellow? The parts add to 2, so yellow is ½.
