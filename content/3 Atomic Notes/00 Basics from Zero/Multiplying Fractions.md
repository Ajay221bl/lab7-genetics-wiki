To multiply two fractions, multiply the tops together and the bottoms together: ¾ × ¾ = 9/16.

Why would you ever multiply fractions in a biology lab? Because "and" questions need it. What fraction of kernels are purple **and** smooth? If ¾ are purple, and among those, ¾ are smooth, then you want "¾ of ¾".

Picture a square that is 1 wide and 1 tall. Shade the left ¾ for purple. Now shade the top ¾ for smooth. The patch that is shaded both ways is ¾ wide and ¾ tall, so its area is ¾ × ¾. Count it out on a 4 × 4 grid: the patch covers 3 × 3 = 9 of the 16 little squares. 9/16. That's the 9 in 9 : 3 : 3 : 1.

![[Product rule gives 9-3-3-1.png|700]]

*The chance of purple AND smooth is the area where the two shadings overlap.*

The trap: adding instead of multiplying. ¾ + ¾ = 1.5, which can't be a fraction of anything. "And" with independent events means multiply; you'll meet that rule as the [[Product Rule]].

Try it yourself. What fraction of kernels from RrHh × RrHh are yellow and wrinkled? ¼ × ¼ = 1/16.
