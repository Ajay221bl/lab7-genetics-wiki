Squaring a number means multiplying it by itself (4² = 4 × 4 = 16), and a square root undoes that (√16 = 4).

The little ² is shorthand for "times itself". 3² = 9, 0.75² = 0.5625, and also (−4)² = 16, because a negative times a negative is a positive. That last fact is the whole reason chi-square squares things: squaring wipes out the minus sign, so a gap of −4 and a gap of +4 both count as 16 ([[Why we square the gaps in chi-square]]).

Squaring also makes big numbers much bigger and small numbers only a little bigger: 1² = 1 but 6² = 36. So when chi-square squares the gaps, a big miss counts far more than a small one. That's deliberate: one big surprise should matter more than several tiny wobbles.

The trap: seeing χ² ("chi-square") and thinking you must take a square root at the end. You don't. The ² is part of the statistic's name. The number you add up in the last column **is** χ².

Try it yourself. A gap is O − E = −6.5. What is (O − E)²? (−6.5) × (−6.5) = 42.25, positive.
