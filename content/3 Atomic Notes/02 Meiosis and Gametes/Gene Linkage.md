Gene linkage is the tendency of genes that sit close together on the same chromosome to be inherited together, which breaks the independent-assortment rule.

Think of two beads on one string versus two beads on different strings. Beads on different strings ([[Law of Independent Assortment|independent assortment]]) get sorted separately. Beads close together on the same string travel as a unit, unless the string gets cut and swapped between them ([[Crossing Over|crossing over]]), which is rarer the closer they are.

What would linkage do to ear 5? An RrHh plant (with R and H on one chromosome, r and h on the other) would make mostly RH and rh gametes and few Rh or rH. You'd see too many purple-smooth and yellow-wrinkled kernels and too few of the mixed types, so the counts wouldn't fit 9 : 3 : 3 : 1, and chi-square would reject.

That's why the answer key lists "the two genes don't assort independently (they are linked)" as the H₂ for ear 5.

Try it yourself. Ear 5's χ² was 0.376 (fail to reject 9 : 3 : 3 : 1). What does that suggest about linkage? Nothing detectable: the color and shape genes behave as if they assort independently.
