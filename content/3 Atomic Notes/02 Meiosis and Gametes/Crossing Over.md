Crossing over is when two partner chromosomes swap matching segments during meiosis I, creating chromosomes with new combinations of alleles.

While homologous chromosomes are paired up, they can break at the same spot and swap the pieces, like two people exchanging the same chapter between their copies of a book. A chromosome that carried R and H might come out carrying R and h.

It's one of three sources of [[Genetic Recombination|genetic recombination]], along with independent assortment and random fertilization. Mitochondrial DNA never crosses over, which is why it changes only by mutation.

Try it yourself. Would crossing over make linked genes more or less likely to separate? More likely, if a crossover happens between them.
