> [!abstract] In one sentence
> Crossing over is the exchange of matching segments between homologous chromosomes during meiosis I, creating new combinations of alleles on a chromosome.

> [!example] Explain it like I'm new (the Feynman way)
> Two partner chromosomes swap matching chunks, like two people swapping the same chapter between their copies of a book.

## Build it from first principles

While homologues are paired up in prophase I, they can swap pieces. A chromosome that carried R and H might come out carrying R and h. Crossing over is one of the three sources of [[Genetic Recombination|genetic recombination]] (with independent assortment and fertilization).

Mitochondrial DNA never goes through crossing over, which is why it changes only by mutation.

> [!question]- Test yourself: Does mtDNA undergo crossing over?
> **Answer:** No.
