> [!abstract] In one sentence
> A gamete is a haploid reproductive cell (egg or sperm) that carries one allele of each gene.

> [!example] Explain it like I'm new (the Feynman way)
> A gamete is half a recipe set, one version of each recipe, ready to combine with another half.

## Build it from first principles

- A gamete has **one letter per gene**: R or r, never Rr.
- For two genes, a gamete has one letter of each gene: RH, Rh, rH or rh.
- In a [[Punnett Square|Punnett square]], the gametes go on the **outside** (top and side); the offspring genotypes go **inside**.

Slide 9: 'Outside of the square, each letter represents the possible alleles from each parent (gametes).'

## Where it shows up in Lab 7

The TA meeting flagged gamete-writing as the most common mistake in the dihybrid crosses, 'made with high confidence'.

> [!question]- Test yourself: Is 'Rr' a valid gamete?
> **Answer:** No. A gamete carries one allele per gene: R or r.
