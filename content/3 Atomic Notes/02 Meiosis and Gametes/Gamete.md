A gamete is a haploid reproductive cell, an egg or a sperm, carrying exactly one allele of each gene.

A gamete is half a recipe set: one version of every recipe, waiting to combine with another half. So:

- One gene: a gamete is a single letter, R or r. Never "Rr".
- Two genes: one letter of each gene, RH, Rh, rH or rh. Never "Rr" or "Hh".

On a [[Punnett Square|Punnett square]], gametes go on the **outside** and offspring genotypes go **inside**. Slide 9: "Outside of the square, each letter represents the possible alleles from each parent (gametes)."

The trap, flagged in the TA meeting as the single most common mistake, made "with high confidence": writing dihybrid gametes as Rr and Hh. Each gamete needs one color letter **and** one shape letter. See [[Calc 02 - Writing Gametes]] and the [[FOIL Method]].

Try it yourself. List the gametes of an RRHh plant. RH and Rh.
