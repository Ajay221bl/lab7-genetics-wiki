Meiosis is the cell division that makes eggs and sperm: it separates each chromosome pair so that every gamete gets one chromosome of each pair, and so one allele of each gene.

Picture a dealer holding pairs of cards: two "kernel color" cards, two "kernel shape" cards and so on. To make a gamete, the dealer must hand out exactly one card from each pair, and which one goes into a given gamete is a coin flip. That's meiosis.

Step by step, for a plant that is Gg:

1. The plant's cell has G on one chromosome and g on its partner.
2. In **meiosis I**, the partner chromosomes pair up, line up in the middle and get pulled to opposite sides. G goes one way, g the other.
3. **Meiosis II** splits the copied chromosomes once more, giving four [[Haploid|haploid]] cells.
4. Result: half the gametes carry G and half carry g.

![[Meiosis sorts alleles.png|700]]

*A Gg cell's two chromosomes are separated, so half the gametes carry G and half carry g.*

Two of Mendel's rules drop straight out of this: the [[Law of Segregation]] (one allele per gene per gamete) and the [[Law of Independent Assortment]] (different chromosome pairs are dealt independently).

The manual (p. 123) puts it plainly: "The most important thing to remember when performing Punnett square crosses is how meiosis and sexual reproduction work." The TA meeting suggests linking to Lab 6 by putting the egg from Mom along the top of the Punnett square and the sperm from Dad down the side.

Try it yourself. A plant is RR. What gametes can it make? Only R, because both chromosomes carry R.
