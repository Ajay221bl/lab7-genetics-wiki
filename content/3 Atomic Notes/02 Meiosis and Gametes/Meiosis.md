> [!abstract] In one sentence
> Meiosis is the type of cell division that makes eggs and sperm: it separates each pair of homologous chromosomes so every gamete gets one chromosome of each pair, and therefore one allele of each gene.

> [!example] Explain it like I'm new (the Feynman way)
> Meiosis is like dealing cards from a two-deck set so that each player (gamete) gets exactly one card of each kind, chosen at random from the pair.

## Build it from first principles

Meiosis is the bridge between last week's lab and this one.

1. Before meiosis, a diploid cell has two alleles of each gene (say G and g), one on each homologue.
2. In **meiosis I** the homologous chromosomes pair up, line up in the middle of the cell, and are pulled to opposite sides. The G-chromosome goes one way, the g-chromosome the other.
3. **Meiosis II** splits the copied chromosomes again, giving four [[Haploid|haploid]] cells.
4. Result: half the gametes carry **G**, half carry **g**. Which one a given egg or sperm gets is chance.

Two rules fall straight out of this mechanism: the [[Law of Segregation]] (one allele per gene per gamete) and the [[Law of Independent Assortment]] (different chromosome pairs line up independently).

## Where it shows up in Lab 7

Manual p. 123: 'The most important thing to remember when performing Punnett square crosses is how meiosis and sexual reproduction work.' In the TA meeting, instructors are told to draw the egg from mom along the top and the sperm from dad down the side of the square to connect to Lab 6.

> [!question]- Test yourself: A Gg plant makes gametes. What fraction carry g?
> **Answer:** Half, because meiosis separates the two alleles into different gametes.
