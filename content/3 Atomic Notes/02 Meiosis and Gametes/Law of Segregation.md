> [!abstract] In one sentence
> The law of segregation says the two alleles of a gene separate during gamete formation, so each gamete receives only one allele of each gene.

> [!example] Explain it like I'm new (the Feynman way)
> Each parent has two cards per gene and puts exactly one, at random, into each gamete. The cards never melt together.

## Build it from first principles

Mendel deduced this from pea crosses in the 1860s, decades before anyone saw chromosomes. Today we know the mechanism: in [[Meiosis|meiosis I]] the two [[Homologous Chromosomes|homologous chromosomes]] go to different cells.

Consequences you use every time:
- An **Rr** parent makes ½ R gametes and ½ r gametes.
- An **RR** parent makes only R gametes.
- Alleles don't blend or get lost: cross two Rr (purple) parents and yellow (rr) reappears in about ¼ of the [[F2 Generation|F₂]]. That reappearance is the proof.

> [!question]- Test yourself: How does Rr × Rr prove alleles don't blend away?
> **Answer:** Yellow (rr) reappears in about ¼ of the offspring.
