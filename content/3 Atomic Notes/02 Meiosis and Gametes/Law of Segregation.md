The law of segregation says the two alleles of a gene separate when gametes form, so each gamete gets just one, chosen at random.

Mendel figured this out in the 1860s from pea counts alone, without ever seeing a chromosome. We now know why it's true: in [[Meiosis|meiosis I]], the two homologous chromosomes go to different cells.

What follows from it:
- An Rr parent makes ½ R gametes and ½ r gametes.
- An RR parent makes only R gametes.
- **Alleles don't blend or disappear.** Cross two Rr purple plants and yellow (rr) comes back in about a quarter of the offspring ([[Why the recessive trait comes back in the F2]]).

Try it yourself. Which ratio did Mendel see that led him here? 3 : 1 in the second generation.
