A sperm is the male gamete: a small, mobile haploid cell that delivers one set of chromosomes to the egg and almost nothing else.

It's stripped down to the essentials: a head (the chromosomes), a few mitochondria for power, and a tail. Any of its mitochondria that do get into the egg are usually destroyed (manual p. 123).

In corn, the male gametes travel in [[Pollen|pollen]] grains.

A TA-meeting tip: students find the word awkward at first. Say it plainly and often, and they get used to it.

Try it yourself. In a Punnett square drawn the TA way, where do the sperm go? Down the side.
