> [!abstract] In one sentence
> A sperm is the male gamete: a small, mobile haploid cell that delivers one set of chromosomes to the egg and contributes almost no cytoplasm.

> [!example] Explain it like I'm new (the Feynman way)
> A sperm is a courier on a scooter: it carries one envelope (chromosomes) and little else.

## Build it from first principles

A sperm is stripped down to a nucleus, a few mitochondria to power its tail, and the tail itself. At fertilization it delivers its chromosomes; any of its mitochondria that get in are usually destroyed by the egg (manual p. 123).

In plants, the equivalent male gamete is carried in a [[Pollen|pollen]] grain.

TA meeting tip: students find the word awkward; say it plainly and often and they get used to it.

> [!question]- Test yourself: What does the sperm contribute to the zygote?
> **Answer:** Essentially one set of chromosomes.
