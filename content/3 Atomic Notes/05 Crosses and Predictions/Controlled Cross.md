A controlled cross is a mating where the breeder decides exactly which two parents mate, so the offspring's parents are known.

In corn, that's easy because male and female flowers are separate. Breeders cover the ear's silks with a bag so stray pollen can't land, collect pollen from the chosen father's [[Tassel|tassel]], and dust it onto the silks by hand. Every kernel on that ear then has the same two parents.

The TA meeting stresses that the lab's ears and seedlings come from "strictly controlled crosses" and are expensive. That control is what lets you test a predicted ratio: if any pollen could land, the ear would be a mix of families and no single Punnett square would fit.

Try it yourself. Why bag the ear before hand-pollinating? To stop pollen from other plants fertilizing some of the ovules.
