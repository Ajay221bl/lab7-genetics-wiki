> [!abstract] In one sentence
> Phenotype frequency is the fraction (or percent) of offspring expected to show each phenotype, found by grouping Punnett boxes that look alike.

> [!example] Explain it like I'm new (the Feynman way)
> The share of the pie each look gets.

## Build it from first principles

Rr × Rr: purple boxes (RR, Rr, Rr) = 3 of 4 = ¾ = 75%; yellow (rr) = ¼ = 25%.

In the chi-square table this is the 'Expected Frequency' column. Multiply it by the total counted to get the expected **number** ([[Expected Value|E]]).

> [!question]- Test yourself: Frequency of yellow from Rr × rr?
> **Answer:** ½ (50%).
