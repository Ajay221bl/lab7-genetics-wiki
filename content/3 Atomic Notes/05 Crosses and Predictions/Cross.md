> [!abstract] In one sentence
> A cross is a deliberate mating between two individuals with known (or suspected) genotypes; 'Gg × gg' is read 'Gg crossed with gg'.

> [!example] Explain it like I'm new (the Feynman way)
> A cross is a planned marriage between two genotypes so you can predict and count the kids.

## Build it from first principles

Writing a cross is the starting point of every problem: name the two parents' genotypes, then work out their [[Gamete|gametes]], then combine them in a [[Punnett Square|Punnett square]].

Order doesn't matter for genes on autosomes: Gg × gg gives the same offspring as gg × Gg. (It does matter for [[X-Linked Trait|X-linked]] genes, because mother and father give different sex chromosomes.)

> [!question]- Test yourself: How do you read 'Gg × gg'?
> **Answer:** Gg crossed with gg.
