> [!abstract] In one sentence
> A phenotype is the observable expression of a trait in an organism, produced by its genotype together with environmental influences.

> [!example] Explain it like I'm new (the Feynman way)
> Phenotype is what you can see on the table: the result of your hand of cards (genotype) plus how the game went (environment).

## Build it from first principles

Slide 5: phenotype is 'the physical expression in an organism; result of genotype and environmental influences'.

- Genotype **Rr** → phenotype **purple kernel**.
- Two different genotypes can give the same phenotype: RR and Rr both look purple. That's why phenotype ratios (3 : 1) and genotype ratios (1 : 2 : 1) differ.
- The environment matters too: a plant with green alleles kept in the dark will be pale; human height depends on diet as well as genes (manual p. 122).

In Lab 7 you **count phenotypes** (green vs albino; purple vs yellow; smooth vs wrinkled) and use them to reason backwards to genotypes.

> [!question]- Test yourself: Can two different genotypes have the same phenotype?
> **Answer:** Yes: RR and Rr are both purple under complete dominance.
