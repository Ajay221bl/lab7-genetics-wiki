A phenotype is what an individual actually looks like (or how it measures) for a trait, produced by its genotype together with its environment.

The slides: "the physical expression in an organism; result of genotype and environmental influences". If the genotype is your hand of cards, the phenotype is what shows on the table.

Two things to notice:

- **Different genotypes can look the same.** RR and Rr are both purple, because one working R is enough. That's why the phenotype ratio (3 : 1) differs from the genotype ratio (1 : 2 : 1).
- **Environment matters too.** A green-allele corn plant grown in the dark will be pale. A child with PKU alleles who eats a special diet stays healthy ([[Phenylketonuria]]).

In Lab 7 you only ever count phenotypes: green vs albino, purple vs yellow, smooth vs wrinkled.

Try it yourself. Can you tell an RR kernel from an Rr kernel by looking? No, both are purple. You'd need a [[Test Cross|test cross]].
