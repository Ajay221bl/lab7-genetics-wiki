Heredity is the passing of traits from parents to offspring, and genetics is the science of how it works.

Start with something everyone has noticed: children look like their parents, but not exactly, and siblings differ from each other. Kittens in one litter come out different colors. For most of history nobody knew why. People guessed that parents' traits blended like paint ("blending inheritance"). But blending can't explain why two brown-eyed parents sometimes have a blue-eyed child, or why a trait vanishes for a generation and comes back.

The answer, worked out by [[Gregor Mendel]] and later tied to [[DNA]], is that parents pass on **separate particles of information**, [[Gene|genes]], which keep their identity generation after generation. They can hide, but they don't dissolve. Lab 7 is a guided tour of that idea: you count the offspring of crosses and check whether the counts fit the particle rules.

Try it yourself. If traits really blended like paint, what would two pink snapdragons have? Only pink offspring. In reality they have red, pink and white, so the "paint" never truly mixed.
