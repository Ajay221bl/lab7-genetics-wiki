Gene expression is the two-step process by which a cell reads a gene and builds its protein: DNA is copied into RNA (transcription), and RNA is read to string amino acids together (translation).

Back to the library picture. The master cookbook (DNA) never leaves the [[Nucleus|nucleus]]. When the cell needs a recipe, it makes a photocopy in a slightly different chemical (RNA). That's **transcription**. The photocopy travels out to the kitchen, where a machine called the ribosome reads it three letters at a time and links the matching [[Amino Acid|amino acids]] into a chain. That's **translation**. [[tRNA]] brings the amino acids; [[rRNA]] forms the ribosome.

DNA → RNA → protein → trait. This chain is why a change in DNA can change a trait at all.

Try it yourself. A gene is "expressed" in a purple kernel but not in a yellow one. What's different? In the yellow (rr) kernel, the protein made from the gene doesn't work, so no pigment appears.
