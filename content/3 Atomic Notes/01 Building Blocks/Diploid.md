> [!abstract] In one sentence
> A diploid cell or organism has two sets of chromosomes, one inherited from each parent, so it carries two copies of each gene.

> [!example] Explain it like I'm new (the Feynman way)
> Diploid means you carry two full sets of the cookbook, one from each parent, so every recipe comes in two versions.

## Build it from first principles

Your body cells are diploid (2n = 46 in humans). This is why genotypes have **two letters**: one allele came on the chromosome from the mother, the other on the matching chromosome from the father.

The manual's definitions of [[Homozygous|homozygous]] and [[Heterozygous|heterozygous]] (slide 4) both start with 'a diploid organism…' because the idea only makes sense if there are two copies.

> [!question]- Test yourself: Why does 'heterozygous' only make sense in a diploid?
> **Answer:** You need two copies of a gene to have two different alleles.
