A diploid cell has two complete sets of chromosomes, one set from each parent, so it carries two copies of every gene.

Your body cells are diploid: 2 sets of 23 = 46 chromosomes. Corn body cells are diploid too. This is why genotypes are written with **two letters**.

The slides define [[Homozygous|homozygous]] and [[Heterozygous|heterozygous]] as "a diploid organism with…", and now you can see why: those words only make sense if there are two copies to compare.

Try it yourself. A cell has one copy of each chromosome. Is it diploid? No, that's [[Haploid|haploid]], like an egg or sperm.
