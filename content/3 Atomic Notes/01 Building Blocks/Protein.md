> [!abstract] In one sentence
> A protein is a chain of amino acids, built from a gene's instructions, that folds into a shape that lets it do a specific job in the cell.

> [!example] Explain it like I'm new (the Feynman way)
> Proteins are the cooks and the kitchen tools. Genes are only instructions; proteins actually do the work.

## Build it from first principles

Proteins are the workers of the cell: [[Enzyme|enzymes]] that run chemical reactions, [[Hemoglobin|hemoglobin]] that carries oxygen, structural fibres, receptors, and so on.

The path from gene to trait is: **DNA → RNA → protein → trait**. Change the DNA (a [[Mutation|mutation]]) and you may change one amino acid in the protein, which can change its shape and its job. A single amino-acid change in hemoglobin is the whole difference between normal and [[Sickle Cell Anemia|sickle]] hemoglobin.

> [!question]- Test yourself: Trace the chain from gene to purple kernel.
> **Answer:** R gene → working enzyme (protein) → purple pigment made → purple kernel.
