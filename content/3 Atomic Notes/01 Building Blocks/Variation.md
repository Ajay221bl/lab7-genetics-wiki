Variation means individuals of the same kind differ from one another, and genetic variation is the part of those differences caused by different alleles.

Look at a corn ear: some kernels purple, some yellow, some smooth, some wrinkled, all on one cob from the same two parents. Those differences come from which [[Allele|alleles]] each kernel inherited. Variation has two sources. New alleles are created by [[Mutation|mutation]]. Existing alleles are shuffled into new combinations every generation by [[Meiosis|meiosis]] and [[Fertilization|fertilization]] ([[Genetic Recombination]]).

Why care? Without variation there's nothing to count in Lab 7, and nothing for [[Natural Selection|natural selection]] to act on.

Try it yourself. Two parents produce kernels of four different types. Which process made those four combinations possible? Independent assortment during meiosis, then random fertilization.

See also: [[Mutation]], [[Genetic Recombination]], [[Natural Selection]].
