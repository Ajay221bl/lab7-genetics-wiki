An allele is one version of a gene; different alleles of the same gene differ slightly in their DNA and can give different traits.

The slides call an allele "a form or variety of a gene, result of a mutation". Picture Grandma's chocolate cake recipe. Somewhere in the family, someone copied it with a typo: "1 cup cocoa" became "1 cup cocoon". Now two versions of the recipe circulate. Same dish, same place in the cookbook, different result. Those two versions are alleles.

In corn, long ago a [[Mutation|mutation]] changed the kernel-color gene. Now corn carries a version that makes purple pigment (**R**) and one that doesn't (**r**).

Three facts to hold on to:
- Each individual carries exactly **two** alleles of each gene, one from each parent.
- A population can contain more than two alleles of a gene (blood type has three: [[Multiple Alleles]]).
- The pair you carry is your [[Genotype|genotype]].

The trap: "an allele is a gene". It's a version of a gene. Two alleles of the same gene are written with the same letter (R and r), never different letters.

Try it yourself. Can one person carry three alleles of the blood-type gene? No. Three exist in the population, but each person carries two.
