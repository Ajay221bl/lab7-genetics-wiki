The cytoplasm is everything inside a cell outside the nucleus: the jelly-like fluid plus the organelles floating in it, including mitochondria.

It's the cell's kitchen and factory floor. Proteins get built here, energy gets made here (in [[Mitochondrion|mitochondria]]).

Why it matters in Lab 7: the egg is packed with cytoplasm and the sperm brings almost none. So whatever lives in the cytoplasm, mitochondria included, comes from the mother ([[Maternal Inheritance]]).

Try it yourself. Why does a baby's cytoplasm come almost entirely from the mother? The egg supplies the cytoplasm; the sperm delivers little more than chromosomes.
