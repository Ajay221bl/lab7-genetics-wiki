> [!abstract] In one sentence
> A chromosome is one very long DNA molecule, wrapped around proteins, that carries hundreds to thousands of genes in a fixed order.

> [!example] Explain it like I'm new (the Feynman way)
> If DNA is a cookbook, a chromosome is one volume of the set. You get two copies of every volume: one set from Mom, one from Dad.

## Build it from first principles

A human cell holds about 2 metres of DNA. To fit it into a nucleus and to share it out fairly when a cell divides, the DNA is cut into separate pieces and coiled tightly. Each piece is a chromosome.

- Genes sit along a chromosome like houses on a street, each at its own address (its [[Locus|locus]]).
- Most plant and animal cells are [[Diploid|diploid]]: chromosomes come in matching pairs called [[Homologous Chromosomes|homologous chromosomes]], one from each parent.
- Humans have 23 pairs: 22 pairs of [[Autosome|autosomes]] plus one pair of [[Sex Chromosomes|sex chromosomes]] (XX or XY). Corn has 10 pairs.

## Where it shows up in Lab 7

Last week's lab (meiosis) was about how chromosome pairs are split up into eggs and sperm. Lab 7 tracks what that splitting does to the genes riding on those chromosomes.

> [!question]- Test yourself: How many chromosomes does a human body cell have, and why in pairs?
> **Answer:** 46: 23 pairs, because one chromosome of each pair came from each parent.
