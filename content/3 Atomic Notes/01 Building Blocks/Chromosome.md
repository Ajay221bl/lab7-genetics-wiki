A chromosome is one very long DNA molecule, packed tightly with proteins, carrying hundreds to thousands of genes in a fixed order.

A human cell holds about two metres of DNA. Imagine trying to split two metres of thread fairly between two daughter cells without tangling it. The cell's solution: cut the DNA into separate pieces and coil each one up tight. Each piece is a chromosome.

If DNA is the cookbook, a chromosome is one volume of a multi-volume set. And here's the key fact for all of genetics: **you have two copies of each volume**, one from your mother and one from your father. These matching pairs are [[Homologous Chromosomes|homologous chromosomes]].

So humans have 23 pairs (46 chromosomes): 22 pairs of [[Autosome|autosomes]] plus one pair of [[Sex Chromosomes|sex chromosomes]]. Corn has 10 pairs.

Genes sit along a chromosome like houses along a street, each at its own address, its [[Locus|locus]].

Try it yourself. Why must chromosomes come in pairs for "heterozygous" to make sense? Heterozygous means two different versions of a gene, and you can only have two versions if you have two copies of the chromosome carrying it.
