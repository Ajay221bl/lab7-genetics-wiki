Amino acids are the 20 small building blocks that cells string together, in an order set by a gene, to make proteins.

Think of beads of 20 colors. A gene says which color goes next, bead after bead. The finished string folds up into a shape, and the shape decides what the protein can do. Change one bead and the shape can change. That's exactly what happens in sickle cell: one amino acid in hemoglobin is swapped, and the protein starts sticking to itself ([[Sickle Cell Anemia]]).

Try it yourself. How many amino acids differ between normal and sickle hemoglobin? One.
