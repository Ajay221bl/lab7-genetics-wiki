Sex chromosomes are the pair involved in determining biological sex: most human females are XX and most males are XY.

The X is big and carries many genes that have nothing to do with sex, like genes for color vision and blood clotting. The Y is small and carries few genes, mainly the switch that starts male development.

Here's the consequence that drives everything in [[Sex-Linked Inheritance|sex-linked inheritance]]: a female has two copies of every X gene (a backup), but a male has only one, with nothing matching on the Y. Whatever allele is on his single X shows.

And how is sex decided at conception? The mother always gives an X. The father's sperm carries either an X or a Y, half and half. So each child has a ½ chance of XX ([[Why each birth is an independent event]]).

*A color-blind mother and a normal father: every son gets her Xᵇ and is color-blind; every daughter gets his Xᴮ and is a carrier.*

Try it yourself. Who decides the sex of a child, the egg or the sperm? The sperm: it brings either an X or a Y.
