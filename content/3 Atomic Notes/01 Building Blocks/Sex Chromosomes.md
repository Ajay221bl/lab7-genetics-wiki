> [!abstract] In one sentence
> Sex chromosomes are the pair of chromosomes involved in determining biological sex; in humans most females are XX and most males are XY.

> [!example] Explain it like I'm new (the Feynman way)
> X is a big volume full of recipes; Y is a thin booklet. Females have two X's (a backup copy); males have one X and the thin Y (no backup).

## Build it from first principles

- The **X** chromosome is large and carries many genes unrelated to sex (color vision, blood clotting).
- The **Y** chromosome is small and carries few genes, including the one that triggers male development.

Consequence: a male has only **one** copy of each X gene, with no partner on the Y. Whatever allele is on his single X shows up. This drives every pattern in [[Sex-Linked Inheritance]].

Also: the mother always gives an X; the father gives X or Y with equal chance, so each child has a ½ chance of being XX (see [[Worked - Study Guide Q9 Five Girls]]).

> [!question]- Test yourself: Why does a male show any allele on his X?
> **Answer:** His Y has no matching copy to mask it.
