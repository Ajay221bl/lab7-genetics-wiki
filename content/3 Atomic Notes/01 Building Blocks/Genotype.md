> [!abstract] In one sentence
> A genotype is the genetic makeup of an organism: the specific pair of alleles it carries for a gene, written as two letters such as RR, Rr or rr.

> [!example] Explain it like I'm new (the Feynman way)
> Genotype is the hidden hand of cards you were dealt (two cards per gene). You can't see the cards, only how the hand plays out.

## Build it from first principles

Slide 5: genotype is 'the genetic makeup of an organism'.

Because each individual has two copies of a gene, and each copy is one of the available alleles, the genotype is just the list of those two alleles. With two alleles (R and r) only three genotypes are possible:

| Genotype | Name |
|---|---|
| RR | [[Homozygous Dominant|homozygous dominant]] |
| Rr | [[Heterozygous|heterozygous]] |
| rr | [[Homozygous Recessive|homozygous recessive]] |

For two genes, write both pairs: **RrHh**. The genotype is what you **cannot see directly**; you infer it from [[Phenotype|phenotypes]], crosses and statistics. That inference is exactly what Lab 7 asks you to do.

> [!question]- Test yourself: Name the three genotypes possible with alleles R and r.
> **Answer:** RR, Rr, rr.
