A genotype is the pair of alleles an individual carries for a gene, written as two letters such as RR, Rr or rr.

The slides call it "the genetic makeup of an organism". Picture a card game where every player is dealt two cards per gene, one from each parent. The genotype is your hand. You can't see anyone's cards directly; you only see how the hand plays out (the [[Phenotype|phenotype]]).

With two alleles, R and r, how many hands are possible? Only three:

| Genotype | Name |
|---|---|
| RR | [[Homozygous Dominant\|homozygous dominant]] |
| Rr | [[Heterozygous\|heterozygous]] |
| rr | [[Homozygous Recessive\|homozygous recessive]] |

(Rr and rR are the same hand; we write the capital first.)

For two genes, write both pairs: RrHh.

Here's what Lab 7 is really about: genotypes are hidden, phenotypes are visible. You count phenotypes and use Punnett squares and chi-square to reason backwards to the genotypes of the parents.

Try it yourself. A kernel is yellow. What is its genotype, and how do you know? rr, because yellow only shows when there's no R to make pigment.
