Complete dominance is the pattern where one allele fully hides the other, so the heterozygote looks exactly like the dominant homozygote.

Slide 6: "A dominant gene completely hides the effect of a recessive one." Eye-color example: BB and Bb both have brown eyes; only bb has blue (slide 6) or green (slides 10-12) eyes.

How to recognize it: only **two** phenotypes, and two heterozygotes give a [[3-1 Ratio|3 : 1]] ratio even though the genotype ratio is 1 : 2 : 1.

Why it happens, from first principles: one working allele makes enough of its product for the full trait ([[Why the working allele is usually dominant]]).

Lab 7 examples: purple over yellow kernels, smooth over wrinkled, green over albino seedlings, freckles over none.

Try it yourself. Brown eyes (B) dominant over green (b). Bb × Bb: phenotype frequencies? ¾ brown, ¼ green (slide 12).
