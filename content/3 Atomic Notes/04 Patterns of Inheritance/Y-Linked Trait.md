A Y-linked trait is controlled by a gene on the Y chromosome, so it occurs only in males and passes from father to every son.

The Y carries few genes; the key one triggers male development. The manual's example (p. 122) is a gene for "very hairy ears (in males only!)". That example is old and disputed, but the rule it shows is solid: Y genes go father → son, like a family surname.

Try it yourself. Can a daughter inherit a Y-linked trait? No: she gets her father's X, not his Y.
