A pattern of inheritance is the rule for how two alleles combine into one phenotype, and how a trait passes from parents to children.

Here's the one question that sorts every pattern: **put two different alleles in one body. What does that heterozygote look like?** And a second question: does it matter whether the body is male or female?

| If the heterozygote… | Pattern |
|---|---|
| looks exactly like one parent | [[Complete Dominance]] |
| is in between the two parents | [[Incomplete Dominance]] |
| shows both parents' traits side by side | [[Codominance]] |
| depends on whether it's male or female | [[Sex-Linked Inheritance]] |

![[Three dominance patterns.png|700]]

*The heterozygote (middle) decides the pattern: like one parent, in between, or both at once.*

The study guide (slide 2) says students must "be able to identify patterns of inheritance" from a description, like: white flowers × red flowers give all pink. That's a heterozygote in between, so incomplete dominance.

Try it yourself. Black chicken × white chicken gives all speckled chicks. Which pattern? Codominance: both colors show, unmixed.
