> [!abstract] In one sentence
> A pattern of inheritance describes how the two alleles of a gene combine to produce a phenotype and how the trait passes from parents to offspring.

> [!example] Explain it like I'm new (the Feynman way)
> A pattern of inheritance is the 'rule of combination': what happens when two different alleles share one body?

## Build it from first principles

The question every pattern answers: **what does the heterozygote look like, and does sex matter?**

| Pattern | Heterozygote looks like | Clue in a question |
|---|---|---|
| [[Complete Dominance]] | the dominant parent | only two phenotypes |
| [[Incomplete Dominance]] | something in between | a blend (pink, gray, wavy) |
| [[Codominance]] | both parents at once | both show, unmixed (speckled, AB) |
| [[Sex-Linked Inheritance]] | depends on sex | far more common in males |

Study guide (slide 2): 'Be able to identify patterns of inheritance.'

> [!question]- Test yourself: What single question identifies the pattern?
> **Answer:** What does the heterozygote look like (and does sex matter)?
