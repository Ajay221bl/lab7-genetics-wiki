An autosomal dominant disorder is caused by an allele on an autosome that causes disease even in one copy (Aa or AA).

One faulty copy is enough, like one bad ingredient spoiling a dish. An affected Aa parent and an unaffected aa partner have a ½ chance with each child. An affected AA parent passes it to every child (study guide Q5c).

Examples (Q5b): [[Huntington Disease]], [[Marfan Syndrome]], [[Achondroplasia]], [[Neurofibromatosis]].

Try it yourself. Can an autosomal dominant disorder skip a generation? Normally no: anyone with the allele is affected, so it shows in every generation it's passed through.
