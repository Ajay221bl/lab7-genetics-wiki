Red-green color blindness is an X-linked recessive trait in which one type of color sensor (cone) in the eye doesn't work normally.

About 8% of men but under 1% of women have it. Why the gap? A man needs only one Xᵇ; a woman needs two, and the chance of inheriting two is much smaller ([[Why X-linked traits show up more in males]]). Slides 13-14 and study guide Q7.

Try it yourself. A color-blind woman has a son. Is he color-blind? Yes: he must get one of her X's, and both are Xᵇ.
