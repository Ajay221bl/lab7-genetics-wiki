Malaria is a disease caused by a parasite (Plasmodium), spread by mosquitoes, that lives inside red blood cells.

It's the reason the sickle allele is common in parts of Africa, the Middle East and India: carriers survive malaria better, so they leave more children ([[Why the sickle cell allele survives]]).

Try it yourself. Where would you expect the sickle allele to be rare? Where malaria has never been common.

See also: [[Heterozygote Advantage]], [[Natural Selection]].
