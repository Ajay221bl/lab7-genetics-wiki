> [!abstract] In one sentence
> A genetic disorder is a disease caused by an allele that does not work properly; a person who has it is 'affected', one who doesn't is 'normal' or 'unaffected'.

> [!example] Explain it like I'm new (the Feynman way)
> A disorder is just a trait we'd rather not have; it follows exactly the same inheritance rules as kernel color.

## Build it from first principles

Disorders follow the same rules as any trait. The key questions: is the gene on an [[Autosome|autosome]] or the X, and is the disorder allele dominant or recessive?

- [[Autosomal Recessive Disorder]] — two copies needed (cystic fibrosis, sickle cell anemia, Tay-Sachs, PKU).
- [[Autosomal Dominant Disorder]] — one copy enough (Huntington, Marfan, achondroplasia, neurofibromatosis).
- [[X-Linked Trait|X-linked recessive]] — mostly males (color blindness, hemophilia).
- [[Mitochondrial DNA|Mitochondrial]] — from the mother (LHON, nonsyndromic deafness).

> [!question]- Test yourself: What two questions classify a disorder's inheritance?
> **Answer:** Autosome or X? Dominant or recessive?
