A genetic disorder is a disease caused by an allele that doesn't work properly; someone who has it is "affected", someone who doesn't is "unaffected" or "normal".

Here's the reassuring part: a disease allele is just an allele. It follows exactly the same Punnett-square rules as kernel color. To place any disorder, ask two questions: **where** is the gene (an autosome or the X?), and **how many copies** does it take (one = dominant, two = recessive)?

| Type | Examples from the study guide |
|---|---|
| [[Autosomal Recessive Disorder]] | cystic fibrosis, sickle cell anemia, Tay-Sachs, PKU |
| [[Autosomal Dominant Disorder]] | Huntington, Marfan, achondroplasia, neurofibromatosis |
| [[X-Linked Trait\|X-linked recessive]] | color blindness, hemophilia |
| [[Mitochondrial DNA\|mitochondrial]] | Leber hereditary optic neuropathy, nonsyndromic deafness |

Try it yourself. A disorder appears equally in men and women and needs two copies. What type? Autosomal recessive.
